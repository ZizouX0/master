# 06 — The four to write to first

Ranked on the two things you actually asked for: **will they reply**, and **will they tell you
you qualify**. Everything here is quoted from the verification notes in this repo's own data —
not an impression.

The short version: **private Spanish universities running *títulos propios*.** Their admissions
offices are commercial, they answer within a day or two, admission is rolling rather than by
committee, and several of them publish an entry requirement that a software-engineering degree
already meets. That combination does not exist at the conservatoires or at Berklee.

---

## 🥇 1. ENTI–UB, Barcelona — *Máster en Música y Sonido para Experiencias del Entretenimiento*

**Write to this one first.** It is the easiest door *and* the only one where your degree is an
advantage rather than something to be explained away.

- **Entry requirement, verbatim from their live page:** *"Si bien no hay requisitos mínimos de
  estudios previos, sí que es necesario pasar una entrevista con el director académico del
  máster para acceder."* — **no minimum prior studies at all.**
- **Target intake names *desarrolladores* — developers — explicitly**, alongside musicians and
  producers. Your data's note: *"his BSc is an asset here rather than something to be excused."*
- **How you make contact: you email the academic director and ask for the interview.** That is
  the published admissions process. It means your correspondence is with a named academic, not
  a call centre — which is the strongest kind of thread to put in a visa file.
- Rolling admission, no deadline, no portfolio, no audition, no IELTS.
- Título propio **issued by the Universitat de Barcelona** — a real, well-known university name
  on the reply.

⚠️ Taught 100% in Spanish, and no required level is published. Ask the director what he expects
— that question alone is a good reason to be in touch, and his answer feeds straight into your
study plan.

## 🥈 2. UCAM Murcia — *Máster de Formación Permanente en Producción Musical*

**The most quotable "yes" available to you**, because their own brochure names your degree:

> open to holders of an official university degree, *"de forma especial se dirige a egresados
> en … **INGENIERÍAS EN TELECOMUNICACIONES E INFORMÁTICA**"* — and explicitly **"sin necesidad
> de homologación de sus títulos."**

No portfolio, no audition, rolling admission through their own portal. €4,194 all-in, verified
to the cent — the best value in the whole Spanish slice.

⚠️ **Two catches.** Only **12 places**, so it is first-come. And it is **semipresencial** — fine
as correspondence evidence, but do not name it as your itinerary's endpoint until they confirm
presencial hours in writing (see the [README](./README.md)).

## 🥉 3. Universidad Nebrija, Madrid — *Máster en Radio, Podcast y Audio Digital*

The **highest-probability yes on the list.** The published requirement is:

> *"Es requisito indispensable … estar en posesión del título de Licenciado o equivalente"* —
> any completed bachelor's degree. Nothing else.

No portfolio, no audition, no test. Candidates from scientific and technical backgrounds are
explicitly welcomed. Rolling, admissions line published (+34 917 800 040). Your data's verdict
is blunt: **"He would be admitted."**

⚠️ It is the wrong programme for where you want to end up — radio and podcast share the
toolchain with music production and nothing else. Use it for the exchange; don't build the
plan on it.

## 4. TAI, Madrid — *Máster en Producción Musical*

**The easiest to physically reach** — TAI publishes a direct admissions phone and address
(+34 91 167 20 62 · `admision@taiarts.com`) and runs a documented process: appointment →
documents → **interview in person or by video** → written admission decision.

That *"written admission decision"* is the part worth having: it is a document, and it comes
before you pay anything.

Named target profiles include **"DJs" and "productores autodidactas"** — self-taught producers.
Rolling; no portfolio published.

⚠️ TAI does not publish the price while it *does* publish a €1,440 non-EU-only fee. Ask for the
full 2027/28 price list in writing before you invest time.

## Don't spend your two weeks on these

| | Why not, for this purpose |
|---|---|
| **Berklee Valencia** | A real screening gate: three original compositions with written scores, a video, a recommendation letter, IELTS 7.5, **$150 application fee**. Selective by design |
| **Conservatorios superiores** (ESMUC, RCSMM, CSMV…) | Public, competitive entrance exams, slow bureaucracy. They will not write back quickly and they will not say "you qualify" |
| **La Salle – URL** *(Máster Universitario Oficial)* | An **official** degree means a real admissions committee, not a rolling door. Plus the 18:00–20:00 timetable problem |
| **Universidad Europea de Madrid** | Their *Máster en Producción Creativa de la Música* has no live programme page and **appears no longer to be running**. You will find it on Google. Don't |
| **UFV Madrid** | 30 ECTS over three months — **not a master at all**, and 10 contact hours/week fails the visa test twice over |

## The honest read on all this

**The features that make these easy are the same features you should be clear-eyed about.**
Rolling admission, no portfolio, no committee, "any bachelor's qualifies" — that is what a
*título propio* looks like from the applicant's side. Spain's rules (RD 1155/2024) do accept
títulos propios for a study permit, so this does not hurt your visa. But a título propio is
**not an official Spanish degree**: it is not in the RUCT register, and it does not give
access to a doctorate. For the exchange you want, none of that matters. Before you enrol and
pay, it matters a great deal — [`results/DEGREE-CHECK.md`](../results/DEGREE-CHECK.md).

**And a private university's admissions officer is, in part, a salesperson.** They will be warm,
they will reply fast, and they may push you toward a reservation fee. Warmth is not admission.
Take the reply, thank them, file it — and do not send money on the strength of an enthusiastic
email.

## What to actually do this week

1. Send the two-question email from [`05`](./05-correspondencia-universidad.md) to **all four**,
   to two addresses each. It is the same email — write it once.
2. For **ENTI**, change the ask: request the interview with the academic director. That is their
   published route, and an interview arranged by email is a stronger thread than a Q&A.
3. For **UCAM**, add one line: *"¿Podrían confirmarme el número de horas lectivas presenciales
   semanales del programa?"* You need that answer for a different reason than the others.
4. Expect the first reply in **24–72 hours** from Nebrija or TAI, and slower from ENTI, because
   an academic director answers his own mail.
