# 20 — What the regulation actually says, against what the internet says

A sweep of Reddit, Spanish/French/Arabic forums, Maghreb press and the BOE turned up **real,
checkable errors** in what circulates online — including in paid agency guides. All of the below is
read directly from the consolidated text of
[RD 1155/2024](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-24099).

---

## ✏️ Correction 1: it is not a *prórroga*, and that changes everything

Moving from a language course to a master's is **not** an extension. A language course is an
*actividad formativa* (**art. 52.1.e.2º**); a master's is *estudios superiores* (**art. 52.1.a**).
A *prórroga* extends the **same** activity. This is a **new initial authorisation** — a different
procedure with different rules.

**And art. 54.1 says it can be filed from inside Spain:**

> *"En los supuestos previstos en el artículo 52.1.a) … la solicitud **también podrá presentarse en
> España** por la persona extranjera siempre que se encuentre **regularmente en territorio
> nacional**."*

Higher education is 52.1.a. Someone holding a valid language-course *estancia* is *regularmente en
territorio nacional*. **So the master's application can be lodged without leaving Spain.**

Supporting detail almost nobody mentions — **art. 54.6**: the **university itself** can file
electronically for a student already legally in Spain, with a **15-day** resolution deadline.

## 🚫 Two claims circulating online that are wrong

**"You must leave Spain and reapply from home."** Stated by at least one commercial agency guide.
**Contradicted by art. 54.1 above.**

The confusion has a real root: what May 2025 closed was the **reverse** direction — someone on a
*tourist* stay can no longer apply from inside Spain for a **language-course** authorisation,
because 52.1.e.2º is absent from art. 54.1's in-Spain list. That restriction is real and correctly
reported in Maghreb sources. It has then been over-generalised into "language students must go
home", which does not follow.

**"You must have PASSED DELE/SIELE to renew."** Stated by two commercial sources. **Art. 55.5 says
otherwise** — the language-course prórroga accepts having passed the EOI course, *or* holding a
DELE, ***or*** — verbatim — ***"haya realizado la inscripción para realizar la prueba conducente al
mismo"*** (having **registered** to sit it), *or* a SIELE diploma. **Registration alone is written
into the regulation.**

Both sources are agencies with a commercial interest in the situation looking hard. Not bad faith
necessarily — but neither claim survives the text.

**Also worth knowing (art. 55.5):** language track = **one** prórroga, 2 years max. Higher
education = **up to two**, plus an authorisation lasting the official duration of the degree.

## ✏️ Correction 2: the six-month bank history

I have repeatedly told him six months of seasoned bank history is a hard requirement that governs
his whole timeline. **That is not what the sources say.**

**No seasoning period was found anywhere** — not in the regulation, not in any account. What people
consistently report is **recency**: statements covering the last 3–6 months, *including the most
recent month*. One applicant was turned away for exactly that:

> *"I did this step in June, which was too early. When I dropped off my application they said I was
> missing the most recent statement from July."*

**This may unblock his timeline.** If there is no stated minimum seasoning, a September 2027 start
is not necessarily out of reach.

⚠️ **But be careful with this.** Consulates do scrutinise sudden large deposits, and the Maghreb
evidence below shows why. The honest position: **recency is the published rule; showing history is
prudence, not a stated requirement.** Confirm with Tunis.

**And a real correction on the amount** — **art. 35.h.1º**: 100% IPREM = €600/month = **€7,200/year**,
and *"**no se computarán** las cuantías utilizadas o a utilizar para cubrir el coste de los
estudios."* **Tuition does not count toward the €7,200. He needs €7,200 *plus* the course fees.**

*(One exemption: the sustenance requirement drops if accommodation is demonstrably prepaid for the
whole stay — extended in Oct 2025 to free accommodation from a relative up to the second degree,
with proof.)*

## ✅ One structural point in his favour

Spanish legal sources describe the classic refusal ground as *falta de continuidad en los estudios*
— specifically the **"descending scale"** problem, where someone finishes a master and then enrols
in a language school to buy time.

**His direction is ascending — language course, then master's — which is the favourable one.** No
account was found of an upward move being refused as incoherent.

## 🇹🇳 What the Tunisian evidence actually shows: almost nothing

**No first-hand processing-time report for a Spanish student visa from Tunis, Algiers, Casablanca,
Rabat or anywhere in the Maghreb was found. None.** And no Tunisia-specific refusal account.

**Nor was a single first-person account found, in any language, of anyone completing the full chain**
— language visa → language year → authorised for a master's without leaving. Plenty describe step
one. Nobody reachable describes finishing step two and reporting back. **He would be an early
mover** as far as the public record goes.

Two things that *were* found:

**BLS Tunisia lists only short-stay categories.** "Etudiant" appears under *"visa de court terme
(durée maximale de 90 jours)"*, and there are **no long-stay pages at all**. Yet commercial
"study in Spain from Tunisia" pages tell applicants to file at BLS Tunis. **For a course over 90
days that is the wrong visa and probably the wrong channel — confirm directly with the Embassy.**

**Processing times run 2–4 months everywhere there is data**, against a one-month legal deadline
(art. 36.4). Doha 7 weeks and counting; Houston "almost 2 months"; Malaga "4 months". The recurring
bottleneck is **the appointment, not the decision** — and art. 36.1 requires filing at least two
months before the course starts.

## ⚠️ The scam warnings are documented, not rumour

A Moroccan article aimed directly at this audience:

> *"كل ما يقدمونه هو تسجيل وهمي، أو بطاقة دراسية لا تُستخدم أبدا، مما يؤدي في النهاية إلى ضياع المال
> وربما الترحيل"* — "all they provide is a fake registration, or a student card that is never used,
> ending in loss of money and possibly deportation."

Algerian press documents agencies selling *"des écoles non-agréées, des hôtels non-réservés, de faux
billets, des visas trafiqués."* Fake Facebook "visa" pages are named. A Moroccan language-school
owner reportedly took €1,500 advances plus €4,000 in fees.

**The decisive check is legal, and simple.** Art. 52.1.e.2º limits qualifying Spanish courses to
**Escuelas Oficiales de Idiomas** or centres **accredited in Spain by the Instituto Cervantes**, in
person. Everything else is noise. Verify free at
[acreditacion.cervantes.es](https://acreditacion.cervantes.es/).

And one practical warning from a student who paid a full year up front at a chain school and then
endured months of abuse from a teacher: **paying a year in advance removes all your leverage.**

## What is established, and what is not

**Established by the regulation, against the loudest online claims:**
- A master's authorisation **can** be applied for from inside Spain by someone legally present.
- The language prórroga accepts **registration** for a DELE, not only a pass.
- €7,200/year, **tuition excluded** from that figure.

**Established by community accounts:**
- The language-course visa works and is widely used.
- Sponsors are accepted, with a heavy notarised and translated bundle.
- Real processing is 2–4 months.
- Scams targeting Maghreb students are documented in the press.

**Not established at all:**
- No account of anyone completing the full chain.
- **No Maghreb processing-time data whatsoever.**
- No evidence on whether Tunis treats language files with particular suspicion.
- No account of a conditional admission letter being used, or being useful, in a language-course
  file — so any confident claim about its effect, **including mine**, is speculation.
- **No corroborated seasoning period for the funds.**

**The honest summary: the plan is legally sound, and better supported by the regulation than the
internet believes. But the second half of it is unwitnessed in the public record, and the Tunisian
consular picture is blank.** The two highest-value actions: **confirm the national-visa filing
channel with the Embassy in Tunis**, and look inside the Maghreb Facebook and TikTok communities,
where the missing accounts most likely live.
