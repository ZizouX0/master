# 13 — Conditional admission: what actually exists in Spain

## The direct answer

> **The "language year + conditional master's place" pathway does not exist in Spain.
> You have to assemble it yourself, from two institutions that have no relationship.**

Every Spanish *pathway*, *foundation* or *curso preparatorio* programme found is built for
**undergraduate** entry through the PCE/UNEDasiss entrance exam — irrelevant to someone who
already holds a degree. UC3M's International Foundation Program, the only true university-run
foundation year, is explicitly bachelor's-access only and requires B2 **already**.

The programmes marketed in English as *"University Pathway"* — AIL Madrid, IH Madrid, Inhispania
— are run by **language schools, not universities**. What they sell is Spanish tuition plus
*advising*. **None produces a university conditional offer.**

And even where one university runs both halves — **Alcalá + Alcalingua**, **Salamanca + Cursos
Internacionales**, **USC + Cursos Internacionales** — the language centre and the postgraduate
school are administratively separate, and **the language course confers no admission status on
the master's side.**

Spanish master's admission runs on fixed annual cycles with letters issued only on acceptance
into a specific cohort. **There is no mechanism to hold a place a year ahead.**

---

## ⚠️ "Admisión condicionada" in Spain usually means something else

It is a term of art, and it normally means **conditional on finishing your bachelor's degree**
(TFG plus ≤9 ECTS outstanding) — **not** conditional on language. Only two universities were
found whose published rules expressly contemplate admission pending **language** accreditation:

| University | What it says | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| **U. de La Laguna** — [admisión y matrícula condicionada](https://www.ull.es/masteres/en/admision-y-matricula-condicionada/) | Genuine conditional admission **including pending language accreditation** | Only **if places remain** after fully-accredited applicants; you sign a commitment; you must accredit **before the July call** or it is cancelled. Months, not a year |
| **U. de Almería** — [FAQ acceso máster](https://www.ual.es/estudios/gestionesacademicas/acceso/master/vias/faqmaster) | Applicants needing to accredit Spanish *"serán admitidos condicionalmente"* | Timeframe not published — ask |

Everywhere else the language requirement is checked **at application**. UCM and UV confirm this
explicitly.

### Where a letter *is* obtainable — but only after paying

- **U. de Sevilla** runs a dedicated **"Fase Extranjeros"**, and issues the admission letter after
  a **€400 prerreserva** or full enrolment.
- **UB's MDAF master** issues a *carta de aceptación* that *"permite la tramitación del visado
  para estudios"* — after a **€2,120 reservation**.

That is the standard commercial pattern: **the letter follows the deposit.**

---

## 🚨 The consulate finding that matters most

From the **Consulado General de España en Casablanca**, *Requisitos visado de estudios*
(January 2025) — the nearest Maghreb analogue to Tunis, and regional consulates use near-identical
wording. The *carta de admisión* must state the studies, your name, the academic year, the
duration, the **subjects enrolled in**, and the **weekly teaching hours**. And then, in capitals
in the original:

> **"PAGO DE LA MATRÍCULA. SIN EL PAGO DE LA MATRÍCULA NO SE RECOGERÁ EL VISADO."**

**You must have paid the language course tuition before the visa is released.** Not a deposit —
the enrolment. Three consequences:

1. The visa rests on a **real, paid, full-time enrolment** — your Spanish course. **A conditional
   master's letter can never substitute for it.**
2. A master's letter is therefore **supporting evidence of intent only**. That is exactly what you
   want it for, so the goal is achievable — but it is a secondary document, and no amount of it
   compensates for a weak language-school enrolment.
3. **Anyone promising "a letter before payment that gets you the visa" is describing something the
   consulate explicitly does not accept.**

> ⚠️ **Verify against Tunis, not Casablanca.** The requirements are near-identical across the
> region but only the Tunis list governs your application: `emb.tunez@maec.es`, appointments
> `emb.tunez.cit@maec.es`.

### And an honest correction about how much this document is worth

**No official consulate or Ministry guidance says a conditional master's letter is accepted as
supporting evidence.** The consulate checklist has no field for it. Claims that "conditional
letters are accepted" trace to **agency marketing**, not official sources.

The accurate reading: the consulate assesses the **coherence and good faith** of your study plan.
A letter or a genuine reply from a real university is useful as **corroboration of genuine student
intent**. It carries no formal standing and cannot rescue a weak file. Presenting it as an
entitlement would be a mistake.

## 🛑 The agency trap — recognise it and walk away

This is a real, criminally prosecuted problem in Spain, and **Maghreb applicants are a targeted
demographic.**

- **Ministerio del Interior / Policía Nacional** dismantled an organisation falsifying
  *empadronamientos*, bank statements and **training-course certificates** — full packages sold at
  **€5,000–10,000**.
- **"Operación Tetuán", Ceuta:** a network defrauded 50+ Moroccan nationals selling fraudulent
  course enrolments at **€1,400 per matrícula**.
- A 2026 Zaragoza operation made **61 arrests**, having allegedly served 5,000+ people.

**Walk away immediately if you see any of these:**

| Red flag | Why it is disqualifying |
|---|---|
| *"We **obtain** admission letters"* · *"carta de admisión garantizada"* for a fee | Letters are issued by institutions **to admitted students**. A third party "obtaining" one means a fake letter or a sham enrolment |
| A letter offered **without any real application** | The consulate requires it to list subjects and weekly hours — it must match a real enrolment |
| *"Visa guaranteed"* · *"100% approval"* | Nobody can guarantee a sovereign decision. **The single strongest fraud marker** |
| A course sold as an *"entry point"* to live or work in Spain, few classes, no attendance checks | Exactly the profile of the prosecuted Ceuta and Vizcaya schemes |
| Payment to a **personal account** or in cash, no invoice | Institutions invoice and take cards |
| They offer to supply **bank statements, empadronamiento or accommodation proof** as a package | Document fabrication — a criminal offence in which you would be a **participant**, not a victim |
| Claims visa validity but is **not an Instituto Cervantes accredited centre** | Check free at [cervantes.org](https://cervantes.org/es/aprender-ensenar/cursos) |

Even legitimate-looking commercial sites in this space are **recruitment agencies monetising
placements**. Not necessarily fraudulent — but their claims are marketing. Treat them as leads to
verify, never as authority.

**The asymmetry is brutal:** a refusal on suspected fraud is recorded, contaminates future
Schengen applications, and can trigger an entry ban. That risk dwarfs any convenience.

---

## 💡 The question this raises — worth answering before anything else

**A large share of Spanish master's programmes in computing are taught in English and require no
Spanish certification at all.** If an English-taught master's would suit you, then:

- the B2 problem disappears,
- the conditional-admission problem disappears,
- and you could hold a **normal, unconditional admission letter** — a far stronger document than
  anything discussed here.

That is a different plan from the one you are running, and it may not be what you want — a
language year in Spain has its own value, and a master's in 2027 is not the same as one now.
**But it is worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.**

## So what you can actually assemble

1. **The visa basis:** enrol and **pay for** a year-long, ≥20 h/week, in-person Spanish course at
   an Instituto Cervantes-accredited centre. This produces the letter the consulate requires —
   naming the course, dates, subjects and weekly hours — plus the proof of payment without which
   the visa is not released.
2. **Evidence of master's intent**, strongest first:
   - a **normal admission letter** to an English-taught master's;
   - a genuine **conditional admission** from **ULL** or **UAL**, the only two whose rules
     contemplate pending language accreditation — approach their postgraduate offices in writing;
   - failing those, a **written reply from a postgraduate office** confirming your degree gives
     access. Less formal, but authentic and verifiable — and honest.
3. **Start the apostilles now.** Degree, transcript and criminal-record certificate, apostilled and
   sworn-translated into Spanish. Tunisia is a Hague Apostille party, so it is straightforward but
   slow — and it is the most common cause of delay, entirely within your control.
4. **Write the study plan yourself.** Since no institution will link the two stages, **you** make
   the link: a motivation letter stating the language year is preparation for a named master's,
   with both documents attached. **Coherence is what the consulate is actually assessing.**
5. **Deal only with institutions directly.** Every step above can be done without an intermediary.
   If you want professional help, use a licensed *abogado de extranjería* — regulated and
   accountable — never a placement agency.
