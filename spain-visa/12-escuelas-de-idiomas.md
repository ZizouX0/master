# 12 — The language course: verified schools, and the trap that would cost you the visa

This is the document the visa is actually granted for. Everything else in this folder is
supporting evidence; **this is the thing itself.**

---

## 🚨 The 45-minute hour

The legal test is **20 lectivas hours per week**. But a large part of the Spanish-school market
prices a "20-hour" course as **20 lessons of 45 minutes = 15 clock hours.** That course reads as
qualifying on the invoice and **fails the visa test.**

Escuela Montalbán says it openly on its own site — *"20 clases de 45 minutos (= 15 horas de 60
minutos)"* — and therefore sells a **separate** visa course that adds 5 h/week of workshops to
reach *"20 horas de 60 minutos semanales."* **Enforex and don Quijote market in "20 lecciones de
45 minutos"**, which is the risky form.

> **Ask every school, in writing, before paying:**
> *"¿Sus 20 horas semanales son de 60 minutos o de 45? Necesito que la carta de admisión indique
> expresamente horas de 60 minutos."*
>
> If the answer is 45 minutes, the course is 15 hours and it is **useless to you** — however
> good the school is.

## The three worth writing to

**1 · Hispania, Valencia — the safest paperwork.** The only school found that publishes a
**written, dated visa-refusal refund clause** with a one-year claim window. Its hours are
published as 60-minute hours. It is a DELE *and* SIELE exam centre, so you can certify B2
without changing venue. €4,000 for 30 weeks.

**2 · Instituto Andalusí, Málaga — the best value.** €4,120 for **36 weeks** — nothing in
Andalucía comes close per week of tuition. Cervantes-accredited, issues the *carta de aceptación*
with the course programme and accommodation attached, full refund minus €200 on an official
denial. **The one thing to nail down: whether its "20 hours" are 60-minute hours.**

**3 · UC3M Madrid — the cleanest single document.** One enrolment covering **7 Oct 2026 to
9 Jun 2027, 600 hours, €4,000, SIELE registration included** — and the most explicit visa wording
found anywhere: the *carta de admisión* is described as a *"requisito necesario para solicitar el
visado."* No chained modules, no gaps for a consular officer to query. The trade-off is Madrid
rents.

### And the one to avoid despite the name

**CLM Granada.** The obvious choice, and the wrong one. Its qualifying course (CILE) requires
**chaining about nine separate monthly modules** — fragile in front of a consulate that wants one
continuous enrolment — and its refund rule is explicitly hostile: **a visa refusal is not
accepted as grounds for a refund.** If Granada matters to you, use Montalbán or iNMSOL instead.

## The email to a language school

**Asunto:** `Curso anual con visado de estudios — consulta de un estudiante tunecino`

```
Estimados señores:

Soy [NOMBRE Y APELLIDOS], ciudadano tunecino. Deseo cursar un año completo de español
presencial en su centro a partir de [MES/AÑO] y solicitar un visado nacional de estudios
en la Embajada de España en Túnez.

Para poder decidir necesito confirmar cuatro puntos:

1. ¿Las 20 horas lectivas semanales son de 60 minutos o de 45? Necesito que la carta de
   admisión indique expresamente horas de 60 minutos, ya que la normativa exige un mínimo
   de 20 horas semanales presenciales.
2. ¿Qué curso concreto me recomiendan para superar los 90 días de duración, con sus
   fechas de inicio y fin y su precio total?
3. ¿Emiten carta de admisión con mis datos, el número de horas semanales, el carácter
   presencial y las fechas del curso? ¿Gestionan también alojamiento o certificado del
   mismo?
4. ¿Cuál es su política de reembolso en caso de denegación del visado?

Muchas gracias por su tiempo.

Un cordial saludo,
[NOMBRE Y APELLIDOS] · [+216 TELÉFONO] · [CORREO]
```

**Question 4 is not optional.** You are about to send several thousand euros to a school in
another country before you know whether the visa will be granted. Two of the schools above have
a written refund policy; one explicitly refuses. Find out which you are dealing with **before**
you transfer anything.
