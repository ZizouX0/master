# 🧭 Four routes to a master's — with dates

**Written 4 September 2026.** Researched by five parallel agents against Spanish law
(RD 1155/2024), the Distrito Único Andaluz calendar, the Ministry's official register of artistic
degrees, the Embassy of Spain in Tunis, and the funding bodies themselves.

This exists because a Spanish-language course in Alicante came up as an idea. The research says
something different from what the idea assumed — so this document lays out the actual routes and
what each one costs in money and time.

---

## The three findings that decide everything

### 1. 🇪🇸 Spain has no official master's degree in what you want

The Ministry's own register of official *Máster en Enseñanzas Artísticas (Música)* lists **five
programmes nationally**. On the university side there is exactly **one** public official master in
music/sound technology. **There is no official Spanish master's in music production, electronic
music production, or sound design — anywhere in the country.**

Everything sold under those names in Spain — SAE, Microfusa, TAI, CEV, CICE, ENTI-UB, UCAM,
Point Blank Ibiza, ESMUC's *màsters propis* — is *título propio*: **not a degree**.
`DEGREE-CHECK.md` counted **68 Spanish "masters" that are not degrees** against about a dozen real
ones, and that finding was re-verified independently.

### 2. 🇬🇧 The one Spanish option that fits you is taught in English

| Option | Language | Non-EU tuition | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UPF Barcelona — Sound & Music Computing** | **English** | €5,750 | *"S'accepten titulacions de qualsevol àmbit"*; the FAQ says **"the ideal candidate has an undergraduate degree in Computer Science."** No portfolio, no audition |
| UGR/UCA · UMA — Ingeniería Acústica | Spanish | **€821** | Acoustics engineering, not production |
| CSM Valencia — Sonología Aplicada | Spanish | unverified | The one creative option — **12 places**, unpublished entrance exam |
| UPV Gandia — Ingeniería Acústica | Spanish | €4,241 | Same subject, 5× the Andalusian price |

**Spanish B2 does not unlock your field. It unlocks acoustics engineering plus one 12-place
long-shot.** The best-fitting Spanish programme needs no Spanish at all.

### 3. 💸 Nothing funds a language year, and almost nothing funds you in Spain

Spanish universities fund the ex-colonial sphere. That is a structural pattern, not a gap in the

## What a language year actually costs

Self-funded, no scholarship exists, for a ~10-month course in Alicante:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Course (20–28 classes/week, long-duration) | €2,275–2,965 |
| Living (Alicante, €856/mo × 10 — see `SPAIN-COST-OF-LIVING.md`) | €8,560 |
| Visa fee | ~€90 (297.59 TND) |
| Apostilles, sworn translations, medical certificate, insurance | ~€300–500 |
| **Total** | **≈ €11,200–12,100** |

Plus you must **show €600/month for the whole stay** (100% IPREM 2026) as proof of funds — €6,000
for 10 months — *on top of* tuition, which is expressly excluded from that calculation.

### Route 2 — Route 1, plus the language year as a fallback

Run Route 1 in full. Results land **April–July 2027**. If nothing funded comes through, start
Alicante in **September/October 2027** and target **September 2028**.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Sept 2026 – Mar 2027 | Route 1 applications |
| Apr – Jul 2027 | Results |
| **Jul 2027** | Decision point — commit to the language year only if Route 1 failed |
| **6 Sept or 4 Oct 2027** | Course starts (visa docs from ~May 2027) |
| Dec 2027 – Jan 2028 | B2 reached; sit **SIELE** (results ≤3 weeks) |
| **~mid-Jan 2028** | **DUA Fase 1** — the phase reserved for foreign-degree holders |
| Feb – Apr 2028 | Admission; **prórroga from inside Spain** — no embassy queue |
| Sept 2028 | Master's begins |

**This is the version that risks nothing.** The €12,000 is spent only after you know Route 1 failed.

### Route 3 — Language course January 2027, master September 2027

The aggressive version. Start **4 Jan or 1 Feb 2027**, reach B2, enter **Fase 2** in June 2027.

| Classes/week | B2 reached | SIELE certificate | Fase 2 opens 17 Jun 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 Apr 2027 | 21 May 2027 | ✅ 4 weeks' slack |
| 28 | 28 Mar 2027 | 18 Apr 2027 | ✅ comfortable |

Then: admission ~22 July 2027 → enrol 23–27 July → **prórroga from inside Spain** → master's
starts Sept/Oct 2027.

**Why it's risky:**
- Visa documents must start **this week** — the law requires filing ≥2 months before the course
  starts, and the appointment queue in Tunis is undocumented.
- **Fase 2 has no reserved international quota** (Fase 1 does, and Fase 1 closes in January 2027 —
  unreachable, since you would not have B2 yet).
- It runs straight through the Route 1 application season, spending €12,000 before you know
  whether anything funded came through.
- It ends in acoustics engineering or a 12-place entrance exam — **not** your field.

### Route 4 — Language course now, master September 2028

Same cost as Route 3, a year later, far safer on B2 and on admission (Fase 1, with its reserved
places). But it commits the money **before** the Route 1 results are known. Route 2 gets the same
destination for the same money with the information arriving first.

## 📋 If you do go the language route — three things to verify first

1. **Is the school accredited by the Instituto Cervantes?** Under **art. 52.1.e).2º RD 1155/2024**
   a language course only supports a student visa if it is at an *Escuela Oficial de Idiomas* or a
   **centre accredited in Spain by the Instituto Cervantes**, taught **fully in person**. This is a
   go/no-go. Ask in writing.
2. **A school certificate will not be accepted by any university.** FEDELE accredits *schools*, not
   students. Andalusian universities take **DELE** (indefinite validity) or **SIELE** (5 years).
   UGR states plainly that certificates of attendance or course completion are not admitted.
   **Budget for SIELE** — results in ≤3 weeks, versus ~2 months for DELE (~3 for the May and
   November sittings). There is an Instituto Cervantes in Tunis.
3. **Language proof is due at application, not enrolment.** The DUA requires the certificate
   uploaded **with the preinscripción form**. (USAL is the one exception found — it lets you
   preinscribe while awaiting a result.)

## 🇹🇳 Tunisia-specific visa facts worth knowing

- **National student visas are not handled by BLS.** BLS covers Schengen short-stay only. For a
  long-stay student visa, request the appointment by email: **`emb.tunez.cit@maec.es`**
  (Embassy of Spain, 22–24 Avenue Dr. Ernest Conseil, Cité Jardins, 1002 Tunis).
- **Legal processing: 7 days** for the Oficina de Extranjería (negative silence) **+ 1 month** for
  the consulate. File **≥2 months before** the course starts (art. 36.1); plan 4–6 months, because
  the appointment queue is not published.
- **Tunisia uses the apostille**, not consular legalization — in force since **30 March 2018**, and
  **Spain did not object**. Competent authority: **Tunisian notaries** (10 TND, or 20 TND outside
  their district).
- **Criminal record** (*bulletin n° 3*, b3.interieur.gov.tn, ~8 days) is required only for stays
  **over 180 days**, apostilled and translated. Assume a 3-month validity window.
- **Proof of funds: €600/month** (100% IPREM 2026, frozen for a 4th year). Tuition is **excluded**
  from this — you fund it separately. Agency sites quoting €700 are wrong.
- **Proof of paid matrícula is now required**, not just an acceptance letter (Instrucción SEM
  3/2025).
- **TIE within one month of arrival** for any stay over 180 days.
- **Visa fee ≈ €90** (297.59 TND), plus ~€17 if routed through BLS.
- A language-course authorization lasts **max 1 year and allows only ONE extension**, which
  requires having passed the course or obtained/registered for **DELE or SIELE** (art. 55.5) — so
  the law itself expects the progression story.
- **Context, not a prediction:** Spain's Tunis post refused **30.1%** of *Schengen short-stay*
  applications in 2024, against a Schengen-wide average of 14.8%. Spain publishes **no** refusal
  statistics for national student visas anywhere, so this is an indicator of scrutiny at the post,
  **not** a student-visa refusal rate.

## Confidence

**Confirmed from primary sources:** the Ministry's register of official music master's; UPF, UGR,
UCA, UMA, UPV tuition and language of instruction; RD 1155/2024 articles on eligibility, processing
deadlines, IPREM and the TIE; Tunisia's apostille status and competent authority; the DUA
two-stage translation rule and the *"sin necesidad de homologación"* provision; the exclusion of
Tunisia from Fundación Carolina and the civil-servant restriction on AECID; WAVES' suspension;
DELE/SIELE result timings and the accepted-certificate tables.

**Projected:** all 2027/28 admission dates. The DUA has run Fase 1 in the second half of January
for several consecutive cycles; the 2027/28 Acuerdo should appear in **BOJA around 20–23 December
2026**. Verify before committing.

**Could not be established:** the national-visa appointment waiting time in Tunis, actual
processing times for Tunisia, and apostille turnaround — no official, forum or agency figure
exists publicly for any of these. The 4–6 month planning buffer is inference, not data.

**Benchmark, not a promise:** the B2 timeline uses Instituto Cervantes' own structure (B1 = 120
teaching hours, B2 = 180, of 50 minutes) converted to 45-minute classes = ~333 classes from a
completed A2. That is classroom time assuming steady progress, not a guarantee.
