# Is it actually a master's degree?

You asked for master's degrees, not certificates. Every one of the 1,091 records was
re-checked against what it actually awards. This file is the result — and the list of
things to **not** pay for.

---

## The count

| Verdict | Records | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| **Master's degree** | 1300 | **A real second-cycle degree.** M.Sc., M.A., M.Mus., M.Phil., mestrado, laurea magistrale / diploma accademico di II livello, máster universitario oficial, magisterský, yüksek lisans. This is what you want. |
| **Master di I livello (60 CFU)** | 10 | Italian **first-level master** — a one-year certificate taken *after* a bachelor. It is not a second cycle, it does not lead to a PhD, and Italy itself does not count it as a master's degree in the Bologna sense. |

## The trap, country by country

Every country has a word that looks like "master" and is not one. This is what to check
on any page before you believe it:

| Country | The real thing | The look-alike | How to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spain** | *Máster Universitario Oficial* | *Título propio* / *máster propio* — the institution's own title, including at genuine public conservatoires like ESMUC | Search the programme in the government **RUCT** register. If it is not there, it is not official |
| **Italy** | *Laurea magistrale*, or *Diploma Accademico di II livello* (the conservatoire equivalent) | *Master di I livello* — 60 CFU, one year, taken after a bachelor | The page must say **II livello** or **120 CFU**. "Master di I livello" and "60 CFU" both mean no |
| **Portugal** | *Mestrado*, accredited by **A3ES** | *Pós-graduação* — a postgraduate certificate with no degree | Look the course up on the A3ES site. *Pós-graduação* is never a mestrado |
| **Netherlands** | **NVAO**-accredited MA / M.Sc. | **CRKBO** or **NRTO** registration — that is a training-provider quality mark, not degree accreditation | Only NVAO grants degrees. CRKBO on a "Master" page is a red flag |
| **France** | *Diplôme national de master*, or a diploma *conférant le grade de master* (DNSEP, DNSPM) | "Mastère spécialisé", private-school "MBA", certificates | The words **grade de master** or **diplôme national** must appear |
| **Germany / Austria** | M.A., M.Sc., M.Mus. | *Lehrgang* / *Universitätslehrgang* — a continuing-education course | A Lehrgang awards an academic *title*, not always a degree. Ask which |
| **UK / Ireland** | MA, M.Sc., M.Mus., M.Phil. (NFQ 9) | "Advanced Diploma", "Professional Certificate" | Must be validated by a degree-awarding body |

## How this was decided

A record counts as a degree only if it names a second-cycle award — in any of the languages
the search covered — and does **not** carry a disqualifier in the same field.

The hard part was negation. Verified records often describe a real degree by saying what it
is *not*: *"a genuine second-cycle degree, not a Lehrgang"*, *"MESTRADO — a real degree, NOT
a pós-graduação"*. A plain keyword match reads "Lehrgang" and "pós-graduação" and throws the
degree away. Every disqualifier is therefore checked for a preceding negation before it counts.

The classifier was checked against 19 cases whose answer was known from reading the source
pages during the search — TU Ilmenau, HfK Bremen, Kunstuni Linz, Saint Louis Rome, MTU Cork,
Ankara, ČVUT, Žilina and Berklee on the degree side; UCAM, Francisco de Vitoria, Pro Audio
Education, Abbey Road, Mohole and two ESMUC *títulos propios* on the reject side; Cattolica
and IULM as *Master di I livello*. **All 19 classify correctly.**

### The honest limit

This reads what the institution published. It does not re-open the national register for every
one of 1,091 records. For any programme you are about to actually apply to, spend the five
minutes on the official register — **RUCT** (Spain), **A3ES** (Portugal), **NVAO**
(Netherlands), the **MUR** *offerta formativa* (Italy). That check is definitive; this file is
a filter that saves you from doing it 1,091 times.
