# Living in your Alicante house, studying at a private university nearby — what the consulate will accept

**Checked 27 September 2026.** Covers starts from **January 2027 onward** (spring 2027 and autumn 2027).

> **Short answer:** there is **no private university near Alicante with an English master's starting
> in January–April 2027** that qualifies for the visa. If you want to live in your Alicante house, the
> credible choice is **UCAM in Murcia, starting October/November 2027**: about 1 hour by train, and
> the train is covered by a €60/month pass.

---

## 1. The rules that matter

| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Must I live in the same city as the school? | **No.** No rule requires it. | RD 1155/2024, Instrucción SEM 3/2025, Criterios de gestión 23 Jun 2025 |
| Will the consulate see my address? | **Yes.** Casablanca, Nador and New Delhi ask for proof of accommodation *"en la que se indique la dirección"* (showing the address). A house you own in Alicante is good proof. The case must look coherent — you can really attend every day. | consulate checklists |
| Can the distance get me refused? | No official rule or reported refusal found. But the consulate can refuse if it doubts *"la veracidad de los motivos alegados"* (the genuineness of the stated reason for the trip) (art. 28.5.d), and can interview you (art. 27.3). A 1-hour commute is normal; 2.5 hours each way to Madrid looks doubtful. | BOE |
| Which schools count? | Only **universities** (public or private) and **higher arts schools** can offer non-official masters that are valid for the visa. A private school that is not a university does **not** qualify. Foreign-degree schools qualify only if authorised to operate in Spain. | Criterios de gestión §8 and §13 |
| Where do I get my residence card (TIE)? | At the police in the place **where you live** (Alicante). But the study authorisation is decided — and later extended — in the **school's province**. | Policía Nacional; art. 54.3, 55.4 |
| Can I work? | Students can work, but the automatic permission is limited to the **region where the authorisation was granted**, plus bordering towns (art. 57.3). For a Murcia school, that likely means working in Alicante city is not automatic (our reading — confirm). | RD 1155/2024 art. 57.3 |

## 3. The options

### Spring 2027 (January–April) — nothing credible from Alicante

| Option | Why it doesn't work from your Alicante house |
|---|---|
| ESBS, Valencia (February, sports management) | **Not a university** → its "private degree" doesn't qualify for the visa. Also only 12 h/week |
| GASMA, Castellón (Feb–Apr, culinary) | Taught in **Spanish** with English support; ~3 h away |
| UNIE, Madrid (April, official, Mon–Fri 9:00–13:00) | A good programme, but 2.5 h each way, not covered by any pass. Only works if you rent a room in Madrid |
| Schiller / Spain Business School, Madrid (February / March) | Classes end at 21:00; the **last train to Alicante leaves at 20:00** |
| UCJC, Madrid (February) | Campus outside Madrid (Villanueva de la Cañada) — over 3 h each way |
| UPC, Barcelona (February) | ~5 h away |

UCAM, Universidad Europea Valencia and UCV have **no** spring editions.

non-EU students, €100 deposit. The catch: admission and enrolment are in July. See

## 4. How to make the application look solid

1. Pick a school **you can reach in about an hour** — Murcia, Elche or Orihuela.
2. Include proof of your Alicante house (deed / *nota simple*) as the accommodation document.
3. Ask the university for a letter that states **in-person, full-time, the weekly hours, start and end
   dates, and the amount paid**.
4. Add a one-page commute plan: Avant timetable plus the monthly pass.
5. Be ready to explain at interview why you chose that university and how you'll get there every day.
