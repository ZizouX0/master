# 🇪🇸 Spain — cost of living for a student, 15 cities ranked

**One question: where in Spain can you actually afford to study?**
Fifteen university cities, priced line by line, cheapest first.

Researched September 2026 by five parallel agents against Idealista, HousingAnywhere, Numbeo,
the Hostelería de España *menú del día* survey, and the official tariff and tuition decrees of
each transport authority and autonomous community. Every figure below is sourced at the bottom.

## What actually drives the difference

**Rent is the whole story.** Strip out accommodation and the fifteen cities collapse into a band
of €390–€570/month. Food, utilities and phone barely move. The ranking above is, to a first
approximation, a ranking of room prices.

| | Cheapest | Dearest | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| Room | Córdoba €250 | Barcelona €625 | **2.5×** |
| Food | Granada €199 | Barcelona €292 | 1.5× |
| Transport | Salamanca €7 | Bilbao €35 | 5× (but small in absolute terms) |
| Utilities | Salamanca €40 | Barcelona €80 | 2× |
| **Total** | **€696** | **€1,194** | **1.7×** |

Three things the table can't show:

**🏆 Santiago de Compostela is the outlier bargain.** Galicia abolished *grado* tuition in
practice — a 99% *bonificación* means a Spanish undergraduate pays about **€7 a year**. On top of
that, USC's public residences (Burgo das Nacións, Monte da Condesa) are **means-tested from
€62 to €292/month**, board often included. A student who lands one lives on **€450–600/month all
in**, which nothing else on this list approaches. Places are allocated by mid-August.

**⚠️ Málaga is the one to watch.** Rooms rose **+20.6% in 2025** — the sharpest of any city here —
on tourist-flat conversion and the "Google effect". Students quoted in the Spanish press describe
rooms that were €200–250 before the pandemic now going for €400–450. It has moved from a cheap
city to a mid-expensive one within about four years, and the trend has not turned.

**💡 Andalucía's "free tuition" is a half-truth worth understanding.** First year you pay the full
€757. From year two the Junta refunds **99% of the credits you passed at first enrolment** — so
years 2–4 cost about **€7.60**, *provided you pass everything first time*. Fail a subject and you
pay full price for it. Same structure in Córdoba, Granada, Sevilla and Málaga.

## The cheap-city shortlist, with the catch attached

| City | Why it's cheap | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| **Córdoba** | Cheapest rooms in Andalucía (€249, per pisos.com) | Room supply **fell 26% y/y**. Brutal summer heat — August electricity can double |
| **Granada** | €310 rooms, and the **free tapa with every drink** genuinely cuts the food bill | No true student monthly transport pass — you pay per trip |
| **Salamanca** | Cheapest transport in the study (€7.34/mo) and most students walk everywhere | The €7.34 pass **requires empadronamiento** since Jan 2025 |
| **Santiago** | Near-free tuition + means-tested public residences | Atlantic damp is a real year-round heating line. Residence places gone by August |
| **Murcia** | €310 rooms, €8 unlimited UNIBONO pass | Highest tuition of the cheap cities (~€942/yr) |

> discounts lapse, base fares are roughly **2× the numbers above**. Budget the downside.

## Seven things that will cost you money if you don't know them

1. **Advertised rent ≠ what you pay.** Most Spanish room listings say *gastos aparte* — bills
   excluded. Add **€40–80/month**. A €350 room *gastos incluidos* beats a €320 room without.
2. **Searching in September is the expensive mistake.** Signing in June–July typically saves
   **€30–60/month**. By the first week of term, supply in Salamanca, Santiago and Oviedo is gone.
3. **Up-front costs aren't in any monthly figure:** deposit (1–2 months), often an agency fee of
   one month, residence reservation fees, NIE/TIE, health insurance.
4. **Academic-year (10-month) contracts carry a 10–15% premium** over 12-month lets — and
   residences bill over 10 months, so the annual cost is ~10× the monthly, not 12×.
5. **Empadronamiento gates the cheapest deals** — Salamanca's €7.34 pass, Valencia's free EMT for
   under-31s, Granada's Bono Joven, Bilbao's Gazte titles. Register at the town hall on arrival.
6. **Utilities are seasonal, and the annual average hides it.** Valladolid, Salamanca and Bilbao
   spike Dec–Feb on heating; Córdoba, Murcia and Málaga spike Jul–Sep on air conditioning.
7. **Campus geography beats city walkability.** Salamanca and Santiago genuinely allow a €0
   transport budget. Bilbao does not — UPV/EHU's main campus is in Leioa. Oviedo doesn't either
   if your faculty sits at Viesques (Gijón) or Mieres.

## How reliable is this

**Good:** transport tariffs and tuition are read from official operator pages and regional
decrees — those are exact. Room prices come from Idealista and HousingAnywhere quarterly indices
with large samples in the big cities.

**Weaker:** Numbeo's samples are thin in the small cities (Córdoba 8 contributors, Santiago 23,
Salamanca 26) and it produces clear outliers — it puts a Córdoba transport pass at €52 when the
official Aucorsa card is €14–20, and an Oviedo cheap meal at €25 against an official regional
€13.20. Where sources conflicted, official Spanish sources won.

**Systematic bias to know about:** HousingAnywhere runs **15–20% above Idealista** for the same
city and quarter (Bilbao: €528 vs €440) because it lists furnished, mid-term, international-facing
stock. If you rent from abroad through an international platform, budget the higher number; if you
arrive and search locally in Spanish, the lower one is achievable.

**Portal prices are asking prices, not signed contracts.** Rooms in student districts typically
close 10–15% below the city average; city-centre rooms above it.
