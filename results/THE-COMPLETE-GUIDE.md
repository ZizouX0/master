## How to read "chance of acceptance"

Every row carries a **Chance for you** column with three values, plus a **Why** column giving the
actual reason. This is the column that does most of the work, so it matters that you understand
exactly what it is — and what it isn't.

| | Meaning | What triggers it |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Strong** | The published entry rules name your background, or accept any bachelor's | *"any bachelor's degree"*, *"computer science"*, *"engineering"*, *"telecomunicaciones o informática"* |
| 🟡 **Possible** | Plausible, but something needs asking or arguing | *"or related field"*, *"or equivalent"*, hedged wording like *"may qualify — confirm with us"*, or silence on the question |
| 🔴 **Weak** | Something you don't have is required | A music degree, a live audition, professional audio experience, or a teaching language you don't speak |

**What this is not.** It is computed from the entry text each institution publishes — not from an
admissions officer, not from acceptance statistics, and not from your actual application. It is a
filter to save you weeks of reading, not a prediction. **Always read the "Entry requirements" and
"Why that chance" columns before ruling something in or out.**

**How it was checked.** I validated it against a dozen programmes whose answers I already knew, and
it caught real errors in my own logic:

- *Conservatorium van Amsterdam, Immersive Audio* was scoring **Strong** because the word "software"
  appeared in the record. Its actual entry text demands *"a bachelor's degree in Music"* and
  *"significant experience in audio engineering at a professional level."* Now **Weak**
- *HfM Detmold Musikregie* was scoring Strong; it is a top-up on an existing **Tonmeister** bachelor.
  Now **Weak** — while Detmold's *M.Sc. Music Acoustics*, which does list Physics and Acoustics as
  comparable, correctly sits at **Possible**
- Programmes taught in a language you don't speak are **downgraded even when the entry rules are
  wide open** — Ankara accepts literally any bachelor's, but it teaches in Turkish, so it is
  Possible rather than Strong

Across 1,029 records: **401 Strong · 419 Possible · 209 Weak**.

## Public vs private — and the trap that runs through it

**647 public · 277 private · 105 unclear.**

Public almost always means cheaper *and* accredited by default. Private is where the money and the
risk both live — because **in every country surveyed, a private school may legally sell you
something called a "Master" that is not a master's degree.**

| Country | A real degree | What gets sold instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Spain** | *Máster Universitario Oficial* (ANECA, in RUCT) | *Título propio* / *Máster de Formación Permanente* |
| **Italy** | *Laurea magistrale* / *Diploma Accademico di II livello* | *"Master" di I livello* (60 CFU) |
| **Portugal** | *Mestrado* (A3ES-accredited) | *Pós-graduação*, *curso técnico* |
| **Netherlands** | NVAO-accredited MA/MSc | CRKBO "diploma", NRTO certificate |
| **Germany** | State-accredited M.A./M.Sc. | — (the private audio sector is marginal here) |

**The clearest example found:** *Pro Audio Education NL* sells a diploma titled **"Master
Geluidstechniek"** for €5,695. It is not NVAO-accredited, carries no ECTS, and the school itself
places the training at **MBO+/HBO− level — below the bachelor you already hold.**

### Two findings about private education worth keeping

**The Netherlands is structurally closed.** There is **no private NVAO-accredited master in this
field anywhere in the country**, and **no private Dutch audio school is an IND recognised sponsor** —
so none can host your residence permit. Abbey Road Institute Amsterdam charges €18,860–€20,160 for a
non-degree; SAE up to €37,580 for a *bachelor*. Don't build a plan there.

**But "not official" does not automatically mean "no visa"** — I got this wrong initially and the
verification pass corrected me. Under Spain's RD 1155/2024 and the June 2025 management criteria,
**both *títulos oficiales* and *títulos propios* are admissible** for a study permit. What actually
decides it is **study intensity: 91+ days, 90% of credits, and 20 contact hours per week** (or 12
in person for concentrated programmes).

So there are **two separate tests** and you need both:

| Test | Decides | Fails it |
|---|---|---|
| Accreditation | Whether it's a real degree — PhD access, recognition at home | *título propio*, CRKBO diploma, *Master di I livello* |
| Contact hours | **The visa** | Evening courses, part-time, hybrid, online |

This is why UFV Madrid's €6,100 evening certificate (10 hours/week) is legally unusable, and why
UCAM Murcia — extraordinary value at **€4,194 all-in**, with your degree named in its entry profile —
is rated only **C**: it's hybrid, and that's a real visa risk. Get it in writing before paying.

## Honesty and limits

- **Verification status.** Every record was sourced to an official page. A subset was then
  re-checked against that page by a second agent and carries a verdict and rating — that pass is
  still running at the time of writing and has covered the German batch and part of Spain. Where
  verification ran, it changed real things: it corrected TU Ilmenau's language requirement from
  "possibly none" to a firm C1, its application fee from €25 to €75, and it overturned my claim that
  an unofficial Spanish award blocks a student visa. **Treat unverified figures as good starting
  points to confirm, not settled facts.**
- **Blank cells mean the institution publishes nothing.** No figure here was estimated to fill a
  gap. Where I give a range, it's derived from published per-month or per-instalment figures and
  labelled as derived.
- **Dates marked PRIOR CYCLE are last year's**, kept because these calendars repeat closely. Confirm
  each when the 2027/28 cycle opens.
- **Living costs are my own country-level estimates**, not research findings. Treat them as ballpark
  comparisons, not a budget.
- **The "Chance" column is a filter, not a prediction.** It reads published entry text. It cannot
  know how an admissions panel will read your portfolio.
